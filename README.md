# On Fano manifolds with the integral cohomology ring of projective space

By ChatGPT 6 Astra

Fujita conjectured that a Fano manifold whose integral cohomology ring is
that of complex projective space must itself be complex projective space.
This is a weakening of the classical characterization due to Hirzebruch and
Kodaira, Novikov, and Yau, where one assumes that the manifold is
homeomorphic to projective space. The weaker hypothesis does not determine
the Pontryagin classes, so the usual Riemann-Roch argument no longer applies
directly.

Fujita proved the conjecture in dimensions at most five, and the work of
Libgober and Wood and of Debarre gives the corresponding Fano conclusion
through dimension six. We prove the conjecture through dimension ten. We also
prove that, in any dimension, the conjecture holds when the Fano index is
sufficiently large: when the index is at least $n-3$, and also when $n$ is
even and the index is at least $n-7$.

The argument starts from the Kobayashi-Ochiai theorem: the Fano index is at
most $n+1$, and equality characterizes projective space. Riemann-Roch,
Serre duality, and the cohomology ring restrict the possible smaller indices.
For each remaining index, the Hilbert polynomial is expressed in terms of a
small number of dimensions of spaces of sections. Bounds on those dimensions
reduce the problem to finitely many arithmetic possibilities. In odd
dimensions, Euler characteristics of exterior powers of the cotangent bundle
give Chern-number equations; in even dimensions, the signature formula gives
the required equations. The remaining possibilities are excluded by
discriminant tests and congruences. Two higher-dimensional cases require an
additional geometric argument using genus-two curve sections and their
section rings.

Li and Peternell recently proved a related projective-space characterization
under the stronger assumption that the manifold contains a smooth divisor
with homologically trivial complement. Their result concerns Fujita's
compactification conjectures rather than the cohomological conjecture
considered here.
